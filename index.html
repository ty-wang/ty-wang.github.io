Tianyu Wang

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Complex Rheology And Biomechanics (CRAB) Lab at Georgia Tech. I received my PhD in Robotics from Georgia Tech, advised by Prof. Daniel I. Goldman. I received my MS in Mechanical Engineering from Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Prof. Howie Choset, and my BS in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, advised by Prof. Guoying Gu.

My research focuses on bio-inspired robots, mechanical intelligence, and locomotion in complex environments. I am particularly interested in how physical embodiment, compliant body mechanics, and robot-environment interactions can simplify sensing and control, enabling robust locomotion in complex terrestrial and aquatic environments.

My work has been recognized by several honors, including selection as an RSS Pioneer, Rising Star in Mechanical Engineering, NSF CPS Rising Star, Trailblazers in Engineering Fellow, an RSS Best Paper Finalist distinction, and multiple research fellowships and travel awards. I have published papers in journals and conferences including Science, Science Robotics, PNAS, IJRR, RSS, and ICRA. I serve on editoral board and as peer reviewer for academic journals and conferences, have co-organized workshops at major robotics conferences, and my work has been covered by various media outlets.

@article{lin2026optimal,
  title={Optimal swimming with body compliance in an overdamped medium},
  author={Lin, Jianfeng and Wang, Tianyu and Chong, Baxi and Fernandez, Matthew and Xu, Zhaochen and Goldman, Daniel I},
  journal={Physical Review Applied},

  publisher={APS}

This work makes compliant undulatory swimming analytically tractable: by extending Purcell's three-link swimmer with series-connected springs at the joints and deriving the body dynamics with resistive force theory, geometric mechanics can predict how a compliant body will move and identify the gaits that maximize displacement. Experiments on a cable-driven limbless robot in granular media confirm the predictions across a range of programmed, state-dependent compliance, turning compliance into a modelable design parameter that can be exploited for robust movement.

This work shows that a limbless undulatory robot moving through a one-dimensional array of boulders arranged according to the Aubry-André potential undergoes a localization transition strikingly similar to the one seen for quantum waves and cold atoms in the same potential, and a generalized resistive force theory that lets drag anisotropy vary in space reproduces this transition and reveals it to be driven by large fluctuations in the torques needed for self-propulsion.
